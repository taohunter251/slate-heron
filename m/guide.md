Modeling guidance (how the result is judged and what usually goes wrong):

- The module is compared with the target image from four views around the front, plus side and back views. The object's front must face +Z, upright (+Y up), as it would stand or lie in the photo. Build the back, sides and underside as complete solid geometry, never a flat facade.
- Get the overall silhouette and proportions right first: measure the target's width : height : depth and keep them. Then match part counts and placement (legs, handles, buttons, windows, wheels), then colours and materials, then small details.
- Write the main dimensions and positions as named constants at the top of the function, and derive part positions from them, so parts touch exactly and nothing floats or intersects by accident.
- Model only the object. No floor, pedestal, backdrop, shadow plane or label text panel unless it is clearly part of the object.
- Thin parts (chains, wires, rods, frames, straps, blades) need a visible thickness of at least about 1% of the object's size, otherwise they vanish at judging distance.
- Colours: sample the dominant colours of the target and use them as base colours. Metals: metalness 0.6–1.0 with a light base colour and roughness 0.2–0.45, otherwise they render nearly black. Glass and clear plastic: transparent: true with opacity 0.3–0.6, a slight tint, and visible edges or rims (do not use transmission; it renders very slowly). Fabric, wood, stone: roughness 0.6–0.95, metalness 0.
- Openings (cups, bowls, pots, rings, tubes) need real wall thickness: use LatheGeometry with an inner and outer profile, or Shape with holes for ExtrudeGeometry. Lathe and Shape points are THREE.Vector2; TubeGeometry paths are THREE.Vector3 curves.
- Repeated elements (bricks, beads, keys, slats, bolts) should use InstancedMesh or shared geometry and materials; keep below 200 draw calls in total. Rendering time matters: keep the total number of instances below about 2000 and do not simulate fur, grass or hair strand by strand; suggest such surfaces with a texture and a few larger shapes.
- Surface patterns (wood grain, fabric weave, printed designs, stripes, labels) may use a procedural DataTexture of at most 256x256 with simple arithmetic per pixel. Never loop per pixel over large textures or many textures: the module must build in well under five seconds.
- Do not use randomness; use deterministic formulas (for example a small integer hash) for variation.
